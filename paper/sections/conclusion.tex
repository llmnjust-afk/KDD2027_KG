\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \method{}, a training-free, query-adaptive retrieval controller for GraphRAG that decides per query whether to use the graph, how wide the beam should be, and when to stop expanding, using only distributional signals (link confidence, hop-score entropy, top-score trajectory) the pipeline already computes and discards. Sharing one retrieval engine with the fixed baseline lets our ablations isolate each component by construction.

Empirically, on multi-hop reasoning with a sufficiently strong judge \method{} improves both accuracy and cost over the default fixed baseline (7B/2-hop: F1 $+10.3\%$, Hit@1 $+7.0\%$, cost $-23.2\%$, $p{=}0.049$; consistent across three seeds), and on mixed-depth query streams---where no single fixed budget is correct---it matches or exceeds the only admissible fixed baseline at $20$--$24\%$ lower cost. We also report honest boundaries: against a \emph{fully tuned} fixed budget on a single known-depth split it does not dominate, and on trivial queries or weak-judge high-depth settings it trades accuracy for cost. These limits follow the call-count accounting: cost savings hold whenever stopping or skipping fires, but accuracy is preserved only when the pruned hops carry noise. The practical takeaway is a zero-training, drop-in controller that is most valuable exactly where deployments are hardest to tune: heterogeneous query streams of unknown per-query depth.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Larger KGs (Freebase, Wikidata) and frontier models would test whether the gains scale; a calibrated judge could sharpen the entropy and stopping signals; and training-free adaptation from pipeline-internal distributions may transfer to other multi-step LLM pipelines such as agentic tool-use.
