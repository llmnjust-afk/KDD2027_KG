Graph retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) grounds large language model (LLM) answers in structured knowledge graphs through iterative multi-hop retrieval, yet it applies the same expensive retrieval budget to every query regardless of difficulty. Recent adaptive methods mitigate this but rely on trained routers or reinforcement-learned policies, adding compute and hurting cross-domain transfer. We propose \method{}, a \emph{training-free, query-adaptive retrieval controller} that decides, per query and per hop, whether to use the graph at all, how wide the beam should be, and when to stop expanding---using only distributions already computed by the pipeline (entity-linking confidences and LLM relevance scores). \method{} shares a single retrieval engine with the fixed-budget baseline, so the controller is the only variable in our ablations by construction. The central finding of our study is that the effectiveness of training-free adaptation is \emph{judge-dependent}: on a strong 14B judge, \method{} simultaneously improves F1 by $4.7\%$ (0.506$\rightarrow$0.530) and reduces retrieval cost by $15\%$ (1487$\rightarrow$1266 \Toks{}) at 2-hop reasoning, and a counterfactual force-continue analysis confirms the stopping rule is safe (false-stop rate $7.8\%$; stopping \emph{raises} F1 by $+2.1$ over forced continuation). On a weaker 7B judge the per-split headline gain is larger (2-hop F1 $+10.3\%$, cost $-23.2\%$, $p{=}0.049$ paired bootstrap), but on a mixed-depth stream a fully tuned Fixed-$K{=}2,B{=}8$ baseline \emph{dominates} \method{} (F1 $0.474$ vs.\ $0.395$ at comparable cost)---and dominates an Oracle-depth upper bound too, showing that when the judge is noisy, doing fewer hops for \emph{all} queries beats adapting per query. We report this boundary honestly: training-free signal-driven adaptation is valuable when the judge is reliable enough that a flat score trajectory genuinely implies the next hop is uninformative, and is \emph{not} a substitute for budget tuning under a noisy judge. The cost saving transfers to a second KG (WebQSP/Freebase, $-19\%$, F1 trading slightly lower on this denser graph) and to a stronger engine. Ablations isolate each controller component---early stopping drives essentially all of the cost saving---and a noise-corrupted linking study shows the use-graph decision is a controllable cost--accuracy trade-off. We characterize the judge-quality scope condition as the key determinant of when training-free GraphRAG controllers succeed.
