\section{Additional Results Across Judge Scales}
\label{app:scales}

\Cref{tab:depth-14b} reports the depth-adaptation Pareto comparison on the MetaQA mixed stream with the 14B judge ($n{=}150$). The conclusion of \Cref{sec:exp-depth} holds under a stronger judge: a tuned global Fixed-$K{=}2,B{=}8$ (F1 $0.470$ at $1099$ \Toks{}) is non-dominated and beats every depth-adaptive policy and the oracle-depth baseline ($0.444$ at $1001$ \Toks{}). The predictability barrier is thus not an artifact of a weak judge.

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{app:impl}

The generator/judge is Qwen2.5-\{1.5B, 7B, 14B\}-Instruct with greedy decoding (\texttt{max\_new\_tokens}$=64$ for the judge, $32$ for answers). Entity linking and the embedding judge use \texttt{sentence-transformers/\allowbreak all-MiniLM-L6-v2}. The per-hop candidate cap is $c_{\max}{=}16$ (traversal order). The LLM judge prompt batches all candidates at a hop into one call and requests a comma-separated list of integer relevance scores in $[0,100]$; the embedding judge scores each candidate by cosine similarity between the question and the triple's linearized text. On CWQ we use the RoG pre-extracted Freebase subgraph per query as the KG. All comparisons hold the retrieval engine fixed and vary only the adaptation policy, on identical query subsets, so reported differences are attributable to the policy. Cost is measured as mean input tokens per query; the embedding judge incurs zero LLM input tokens.
